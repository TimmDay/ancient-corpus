# Jordanes, Romana: our English translation

Translated from Theodor Mommsen's Latin (1882), as The Latin Library gives it, a section per section of his edition. Made with Claude Opus 5.5 on 19 November 2026 (docs/adr/0003). Only sections 278 to 296 so far, from Macrinus to Diocletian, Aurelian at 290 and 291. Jordanes's Latin is often ungrammatical; the English gives his sense.

## 278

Macrinus, who held the praetorian prefecture, was made emperor and reigned one year, and was killed at Archelais.

## 279

Marcus Aurelius, son of Antoninus Caracalla and priest of the temple of Elagabalus, was made emperor and reigned 4 years. Emmaus in Judaea was built up and named Nicopolis. At that time too Africanus, the distinguished writer on chronology, undertook an embassy to the emperor on its behalf. But the emperor, since he left out no kind of obscenity in his reign that he did not commit, was killed in a mutiny of the soldiers.

## 280

Alexander, the son of Mamaea, of no high birth, took up the government of the kingdom while still a youth, and soon taking up arms against Xerxes, king of the Persians, triumphed wonderfully over the spoils of the Parthians. Under his rule too, at Actian Nicopolis, that is in Epirus, the edition of the divine scriptures called the sixth was found in a jar. He himself was killed at Moguntiacum in a mutiny of the soldiers, and Maximinus, from the ranks of the soldiers, succeeded him in the kingdom.

## 281

Maximinus, a Goth by race, born of a father Micca and an Alan mother Ababa, came to the throne by the will of the soldiers alone. He waged war against the Germans successfully, and returning from there, stirring up an internal war against the Christians, after reigning barely three years he was killed at Aquileia by Pupienus.

## 282

Gordian, made emperor when hardly more than a boy, reigned barely six years. For as soon as he entered Rome he killed Pupienus and Albinus on the spot, who had seized the tyranny after killing Maximinus; and opening the double gate of Janus he set out for the East and made war on the Parthians, and returning from there victorious he was killed by the treachery of Philip, the praetorian prefect, not far from Roman soil.

## 283

Philip entered on the rule shamelessly and reigned 7 years. He made his son, also Philip, partner in the kingdom, and was himself the first of all the emperors to become a Christian; and in the third year of his rule he celebrated the festival of the city of Rome, for the thousandth year it had completed, and rebuilding a city in Thrace called Pulpudeva he named it after himself, Philippopolis.

## 284

Decius, born at Budalia in Lower Pannonia, killed both Philips and reigned one year and three months, and took up arms against the Christians out of hatred of the Philips' name. He himself, with his son, met a cruel death at Abrittus, fighting the Getae.

## 285

Gallus and Volusianus reigned 2 years 4 months. When they had set out from the city against Aemilianus, who was plotting revolution in Moesia, they were killed at Forum Flaminii.

## 286

Aemilianus for his part perished in the third month of the tyranny he had seized.

## 287

Valerian and Gallienus, raised to the rule, the one by the soldiers in Raetia, the other by the senate at Rome, reigned 15 years. Valerian, having stirred up a persecution against the Christians, was at once captured by Shapur, king of the Persians, and grew old there in miserable slavery. Gallienus, seeing his end, gave the Christians peace. But while he ran wild in the kingdom and did nothing manly, the Parthians laid waste Syria and Cilicia, the Germans and Alans plundered the Gauls and came as far as Ravenna, the Goths laid waste Greece, the Quadi and Sarmatians invaded the Pannonias, the Germans again occupied the Spains. For this reason Gallienus was killed at Mediolanum.

## 288

Claudius reigned 1 year 9 months. He attacked the Goths, who had been laying waste Illyricum and Macedonia for 15 years, and destroyed them with incredible slaughter, so that a golden shield was set up for him in the senate house and a golden statue on the Capitol. And he was killed at Sirmium.

## 289

After his death Quintillus, his brother, was hailed Augustus by the senate and was killed at Aquileia on the eighteenth day of his rule.

## 290

Aurelian, a native of Dacia Ripensis, reigned 5 years 6 months. He soon recovered the Gauls at Catalauni, when Tetricus betrayed his own army; and making an expedition to the Danube he routed the Goths in great battles; and he persecuted the worshippers of the divine name. Odenathus of Palmyra, before him, gathering a band of country people, had driven the Persians out of Mesopotamia and seized those places himself.

## 291

When he was killed, his wife held the empire of the East. Aurelian, undertaking an expedition against her, defeated her at Immae, near Antioch, and brought her alive to Rome in his triumph; and then, setting out on a second expedition, he was killed between Byzantium and Heraclea, at Caenophrurium on the old road.

## 292

Tacitus reigned 6 months. When he was killed in Pontus, Florian took up the rule and held it 88 days. And he likewise was killed at Tarsus.

## 293

Probus reigned 6 years 4 months. He allowed the Gauls and Spaniards to have vineyards. At that time Saturninus, master of the soldiers, when he had been sent to restore the city of Antioch, seized the tyranny there, was soon crushed and was killed at Apamea. The emperor Probus himself was killed in a mutiny of the soldiers at Sirmium, in the tower called the Iron Tower.

## 294

Carus reigned with his sons Carinus and Numerian 2 years; he was a native of Narbo in Gaul. He wonderfully laid waste almost all Persia and took their most famous cities, Coche and Ctesiphon. He brought the Sarmatian war to a successful end. Carus himself, while pitching camp above the river Tigris, was struck by lightning and died.

## 295

Numerian, seized by a pain in the eyes, was killed by the plotting of his father-in-law Aper while he was being carried in a litter, and was recognised only on the third day by the stench of the corpse. Carinus was defeated in battle at Margus and killed.

## 296

Diocletian the Dalmatian, the son of a clerk, was chosen emperor and reigned 20 years. As soon as he was raised to the kingdom, he struck Aper down on the spot before an assembly of the soldiers, swearing that Aper had murdered Numerian without any crime of his. And soon he took Maximian Herculius as his partner. This Maximian crushed a multitude of country people, whom they call Bacaudae, and gave the Gauls peace.
