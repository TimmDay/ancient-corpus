After the reign of Severus, Antoninus Geta reigned 1 year. He was short, broad-faced, snub-nosed, balding, greying, pot-bellied, wide-mouthed, fair-skinned, with small eyes. The army did away with him, and he died aged 51.

After the reign of Antoninus Geta, Antoninus Caracalla, son of Severus, reigned 6 years and 22 days. He was tall and strong, of good colour, with a crooked nose, a thick beard, greying, curly-haired. He was loved by the whole people of Rome, and he favoured the Green faction. He was slaughtered in the palace as he was going out to the temple, aged 47.

After the reign of Antoninus Caracalla, Valerian reigned 6 years. He was short, slight, straight-haired, grey, somewhat snub-nosed, thick-bearded, dark, with large eyes, timid, mean. In his reign one of the councillors of Antioch the Great, named Mariades, was expelled from the council by the scheming of the whole council and the people; for he fell short in the horse races, whichever faction he led,

## 296

not buying horses, but pocketing the public funds of the hippodrome. He went off to Persia and offered Shapur, king of the Persians, to betray to him Antioch the Great, his own city. And the same Shapur, king of the Persians, came with a great force of soldiers by the frontier of Chalcis, and took all Syria and plundered it. And he took the great city of Antioch in the evening, and plundered it, and overturned and burned it, in the year 314 of the city's era. And he beheaded the councillor, as a traitor to his own country. He took all the eastern parts too, and overturned and burned and plundered them, and killed everyone, as far as the city of Emesa in Phoenice Libanensis.

And the priest of Aphrodite, named Sampsigeramus, went out with a force of country people and slingers and met him. And Shapur, king of the Persians, seeing the priestly dress, ordered his own army not to shoot at them, nor to attack them, nor to fight them, but received the priest as an envoy; for the priest had sent word ahead asking him to receive him as an envoy for his own country. And while he was talking with the priest, King Shapur seated on a high altar, one of the country people threw a stone with his sling and struck King Shapur on the forehead, and he died at once on the spot. And in the confusion that followed,

## 297

his army heard that he was dead; and thinking the Romans had arrived, they all fled to the frontier, pursued by the country people and the priest Sampsigeramus, leaving all the plunder, and vanished. Enathus, king of the barbarian Saracens, who held the land of Arabia, met them at the frontier, taking the Romans' side. He had a wife named Zenobia, a Saracen queen. And Enathus, king of the Saracens, destroyed all the Persians of Shapur's force, as the wise chronicler Domninus set out.

But the most wise Philostratus wrote otherwise about Shapur, king of the Persians, saying that he took Syria too and burned it, with Antioch the Great and many other cities, and likewise took Cilicia, burning Alexandria the Less and Rhosus and Anazarbus and Aegae and Nicopolis and many other cities of Cilicia, and came down through Cappadocia to the Persian lands; and that Enathus, king of the Saracens, met him, coming, he says, as his ally, and killed him. But Domninus set it out more truly, saying that Shapur sent his satrap Spates against Cilicia with a host.

The emperor Valerian had gone out from Rome to war, to Mediolanum; and he learned about the East, and wished to turn back and go down to the East,

## 298

and could not, but was surrounded and slaughtered there at Mediolanum, aged 61.

After the reign of Valerian, Gallienus, also called Licinianus, reigned 14 years. He was of full height and noble, dark, curly-haired, thick-bearded, with a fine nose, large eyes, great-souled; and he favoured the Blue faction. As soon as he became emperor he marched against the Persians and went down to avenge the Romans; and he gave much to the plundered and the survivors, and raised up what had been burned, and lightened the taxes for 4 years. He built a great temple at Emesa too. And joining battle with the Persians, when many had fallen on both sides, he made peace. And returning from there he went to Arabia, and joined battle with Enathus, king of the barbarian Saracens, and killed him, and took Arabia; and he returned to Rome, and was struck down by disease and died, aged 50.

After the reign of Gallienus, also called Licinianus, Claudius Apollianus reigned 9 years. He was above middle height, fair, pot-bellied, straight-haired, broad-faced, somewhat snub-nosed, with grey-blue eyes, fairish, with a twisted mouth, a slight lisp, great-souled; and he favoured the Green faction. In his reign Nicomedia, the metropolis

## 299

of Bithynia, suffered its fourth disaster from the wrath of God, as far as the rivers and Dacibyza. And he gave generously to the survivors and to the city.

At the same time Zenobia the Saracen, the wife of Enathus, avenging the death of her own husband, took his kinsmen and seized Arabia, then held by the Romans, killing Trassus too, the Romans' dux, and all the force with him, in the reign of the same Apollianus, also called Claudius. The same emperor Claudius was at Sirmium making war, and died there, aged 56.

After the reign of Apollianus Claudius, Quintillianus reigned 17 days. He was above middle height, slight, long-faced, long-nosed, dark, straight-haired, with fine eyes, greying in hair and beard. And he died in the palace, for he was sick when he was proclaimed emperor under dire necessity, aged 41.

After the reign of Quintillianus, the most divine Aurelian, the warlike, reigned 6 years. He was tall, slight, balding, with small eyes, all grey, great-souled, easily angered; and he wore a diadem bearing a star. As soon as he became emperor he began to build the walls of Rome strongly, for they had decayed with time. He himself stood over the work, and compelled the guilds of Rome to serve in the building; and having finished the walls in a very

## 300

short time, he made a divine decree of his that from that time the craftsmen's guilds of the whole city should be called Aureliani, receiving the emperor's name as an honour for their honour and labours.

The same Aurelian, receiving information, marched against Zenobia, queen of the Saracens, and went to the East. For it was reported about her that she had plundered and burned the eastern parts as far as the borders of Antioch the Great, encamped near the river Orontes. As soon as the same emperor Aurelian reached Antioch, he set out at once, just as he was, and went out against her; and engaging her he cut down all her forces. And taking Zenobia herself, he seated her on a dromedary camel; and parading her, he led her into all the eastern lands, and into Antioch the Great. And watching a horse race there, he led her into the hippodrome on the dromedary camel. And building a platform in that Antioch, he stood her on it, bound, for three days; and he called the place he built the Triumph. And from there he took her down and led her away to Rome, as queen of the barbarian Saracens; and having led her in triumph in Rome in the same fashion as before, he beheaded her.

The same emperor Aurelian also killed all the kinsmen of Enathus the Saracen, the barbarian Saracens who were holding Arabia, and made it a province under the Romans.

## 301

As he was about to leave Antioch the Great, the so-called moneyers of Antioch rioted against him, demanding certain customary dues; and he was angered with them and punished them.

The same Aurelian also made the land beside the river, near the Danube, into a province, Dacia. The same Aurelian joined battle in another war, and was treacherously killed by his own army at a place called Caenophrurium, for having led the army badly. And he died aged 61.

After the reign of Aurelian, Tacitus Augustus reigned 6 months. He was above middle height, slight, balding, eloquent, short-haired, all grey, thin-nosed, prudent. In his reign there appeared a Manichaean named Cerdon, teaching his doctrines and gathering crowds. In the reign of the same Tacitus there was a war in Pontus; and the same emperor went down to fight, and was slaughtered at Ztannice in Pontus, aged 75.

After the reign of Tacitus, Florian Augustus reigned 2 months. He was short, stout, bald in front, fair, with wine-dark eyes, somewhat snub-nosed, greying in hair and beard, and very sharp of mind. He marched against

## 302

the Persians, and as he was going down through Tarsus he was slaughtered by his own men, aged 65.

After the reign of Florian, Aelius Probus reigned 3 years and 3 months. He was above middle height, pot-bellied, straight-haired, close-cropped, thick-bearded, dark, ruddy, with fine eyes, very wise; and he favoured the Green faction. In his reign he adorned the so-called Museum at Antioch the Great, and the semicircular Nymphaeum in it, picturing Ocean there in mosaic. The same emperor also set by a divine decree of his an allowance for teachers out of the public funds of the same city of Antioch, so that they should teach free.

The same emperor Probus fought the Goths at Sirmium; and while he was at war there was a great famine over the whole world, and since no provisions were found the army mutinied, and falling on him they slaughtered him at Sirmium, aged 50.

After the reign of Probus, the most divine Carus reigned 2 years. He was short, deep-chested, fair, straight-haired, greying, with grey-blue eyes, broad-faced, fine-nosed, thick-lipped, arrogant. In his reign he subdued a land which he called Caria, making it a province. He marched against the Persians too, and invading took the Persian lands as far as the city of Ctesiphon, and returned. And on the frontier he walled

## 303

a fort, which he made a city, giving it a city's rights too, and called it after his own name, Carae. Returning to Rome, he went out to another war, against the Huns, and was slaughtered in the consulship of Maximus and Januarius, aged 60 and a half.

After the reign of Carus, Numerian Augustus reigned two years. He was tall, slight, straight-haired, long-faced, of fine features, well-bearded, greying, with a fine nose and fine eyes, dark. In his reign there was a great persecution of the Christians, in which the holy George the Cappadocian was martyred, and the holy Babylas; for he was bishop of Antioch the Great. And the same emperor Numerian arrived there, on his way to make war on the Persians; and wishing to spy out the divine mysteries of the Christians, he wanted to go into the holy church where the Christians gathered, to see what the mysteries were that they performed, having heard that these Galileans perform their liturgies in secret. And as he came near, he was suddenly met by the holy Babylas; and he stopped him, saying to him, "You are defiled by the sacrifices to idols, and I do not allow you to see the mysteries of the living God."
