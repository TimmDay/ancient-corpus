# Polemius Silvius, Laterculus I: our English translation

Translated from Theodor Mommsen's Latin (Chronica Minora 1, 1892), as this project typed it from his pages, a section per entry. Made with Claude Opus 5.5 on 19 November 2026 (docs/adr/0003). "Usurper" renders *tyrannus*. The list was finished in 449, the consulship of Postumianus and Zeno.

## 1

In the seven hundred and tenth year from the founding of the city, Gaius Julius Caesar, Pompey's father-in-law, first made himself emperor, from dictator.

## 2

When he was killed in the senate house, after four years Lepidus, Antony and Octavian, the grandson of the aforesaid Caesar's sister through her daughter, were appointed triumvirs. Of these, when Lepidus had died, and he had defeated Antony, the husband of Queen Cleopatra, in a sea battle, the aforesaid Octavian, first called Augustus, held the empire alone for fifty-six years.

## 3

Under him the Caesars Gaius and Lucius perished by different fates of death.

## 4

He was succeeded by Tiberius, his stepson by Livia, whom the same Augustus had joined to himself in marriage while she was pregnant and her husband Domitius still alive.

## 5

Gaius Caligula, son of Germanicus, killed by Chaerea.

## 6

Claudius, Gaius's uncle and father of Britannicus.

## 7

Under him Camillus first became a usurper, in Syria.

## 8

Nero, son of Ahenobarbus and Agrippina, who in his fifteenth year killed himself with the sword, when for his crimes and disgraces, in which he outdid the whole human race, he was sought by the Roman people for punishment.

## 9

Under him Vindex and Clodius were usurpers.

## 10

Galba killed with Piso.

## 11

Vespasian.

## 12

Titus his son, subduer of the Jewish nation.

## 13

Domitian his brother, who was the first Flavian to order himself to be called lord; killed by Stephanus.

## 14

Under him Antonius was a usurper.

## 15

Nerva, from prefect.

## 16

Trajan Ulpius.

## 17

Hadrian Aelius.

## 18

Antoninus Pius.

## 19

Under him Cassius was a usurper in the East.

## 20

Verus.

## 21

Marcus Aurelius.

## 22

Commodus his son, killed.

## 23

Pertinax killed.

## 24

Julianus killed.

## 25

Severus the African.

## 26

Under him Pescennius, and Albinus from Caesar, were usurpers.

## 27

Geta, son of Severus, killed by his brother.

## 28

Antoninus Caracalla, brother of the aforesaid.

## 29

Macrinus with his son Diadumenus, killed.

## 30

Antoninus Heliogabalus killed.

## 31

Under him Marcellus Caesar, and Sallustius, Uranius, Seleucus and Taurinus were usurpers.

## 32

Alexander.

## 33

Maximinus with his son, killed.

## 34

Under him the two Gordians were usurpers in Africa.

## 35

Balbinus, Pupienus, killed.

## 36

Gordian killed.

## 37

Philip with Philip, who was the first to become a Christian.

## 38

Under him Iotapianus was a usurper in Cappadocia.

## 39

Decius with his son Herennius, killed in battle with the Goths.

## 40

Under him Priscus in Macedonia and Valens at Rome were usurpers.

## 41

Hostilianus with Volusianus Caesar.

## 42

Aemilianus.

## 43

Valerian, captured by the Persians, died among them.

## 44

Gallienus, son of the aforesaid, with his sons Saloninus and Licinius, killed.

## 45

Under him Ingenuus at Sirmium and Regalianus in the same place; at Vienne Postumus, Laelianus and Marius, from a smith; Macrinus too, Quietus and Odaenathus in the East, and Aureolus in Italy were usurpers.

## 46

Claudius killed in the Gothic war.

## 47

Quintillus killed.

## 48

Aurelian killed.

## 49

Under him Victorinus, Vabalathus and his mother Zenobia, and Antiochus; at Rome Felicissimus; the two Tetrici, father and son, who surrendered to him and after the purple were made governors of provinces; and Faustinus at Trier were usurpers.

## 50

Tacitus.

## 51

Florian his brother, killed.

## 52

Probus, who allowed the Gauls to have vineyards.

## 53

Under him Saturninus, Proculus and Bonosus were usurpers.

## 54

Carus struck by lightning in Persia.

## 55

Carinus his son, killed.

## 56

Numerian, brother of the aforesaid.

## 57

Under him Julianus was a usurper.

## 58

Diocletian and Maximian, under whom the Roman empire was first divided; they were the first to lay down the throne of their own will.

## 59

Under them Achilleus in Egypt, Carausius and Allectus in Britain were usurpers.

## 60

Constantius and Galerius.

## 61

Under them Maximinus and Severus were Caesars.

## 62

Constantine, son of Constantius, by whom Crispus Caesar, his son, was killed, and Maxentius, his wife's brother. Under him Alexander was a usurper, and his father-in-law Maximian, when he had taken up the empire again, and Licinius, his sister's husband, who had made Martinianus and Valens Caesars for himself, were put to death at Thessalonica together with Licinius his son. From him the emperors began to be Christian.

## 63

And Calocaerus was a usurper in Cyprus; and Dalmatius, his brother by another mother, of whom were born Gallus and Julian, who became emperor, was made Caesar; Hannibalianus, brother of the aforesaid, was made king of kings of the Pontic peoples.

## 64

Constantine, son of Constantius, killed.

## 65

Constans, brother of the aforesaid, of most infamous life, killed.

## 66

Constantius, brother of the aforesaid.

## 67

Under him Magnentius and Decentius, of the Frankish nation, Nepotianus also at Rome, and Silvanus in Gaul were usurpers; and Gallus his cousin was Caesar, whom he himself ordered killed.

## 68

Julian.

## 69

Jovian.

## 70

Valentinian.

## 71

Valens his brother, burned by the Goths.

## 72

Under him Procopius was a usurper at Antioch.

## 73

Gratian, son of Valentinian. Under him Maximus, and Victor the usurper's son, were usurpers. He was killed at Lugdunum.

## 74

Valentinian, brother of the aforesaid, ended his life by the noose at Vienne.

## 75

Theodosius, made Augustus by Gratian.

## 76

Under him Eugenius was a usurper.

## 77

Arcadius, son of Theodosius.

## 78

Honorius, brother of the aforesaid.

## 79

Under him Gratian and Constantine, Attalus twice, Constans, Maximus and Servatus, Marcus, Magnus and Maximus, Jovinus, Sebastian and Victor were usurpers.

## 80

Constantius.

## 81

Our lord Theodosius, the present Augustus.

## 82

Our lord Placidus Valentinian.

## 83

Under whom the usurper John was put down, and by whom, with our ladies, his mother Placidia and his wife Eudoxia, Augustae, the empire is now held.

## 84

Which I noted down in the consulship of Postumianus and Zeno, men of clarissimus rank.
